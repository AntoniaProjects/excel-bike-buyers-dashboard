# Excel Bike Buyers Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer demographics and purchasing behavior using Microsoft Excel.

The objective was to explore patterns and associations in bicycle purchasing behavior across demographic, income, education, regional, and commuting characteristics using an interactive Excel dashboard.

## Dataset

The dataset contains customer information including:

* Gender
* Marital Status
* Income
* Education
* Occupation
* Age
* Region
* Commute Distance
* Bike Purchase Status

## Tools Used

* Microsoft Excel
* Pivot Tables
* Pivot Charts
* Slicers
* Dashboard Design

## Process

### Data Cleaning

* Standardized categorical values
* Improved data readability
* Created age brackets
* Removed inconsistencies

### Data Analysis

Created Pivot Tables to analyze:

* Income vs Bike Purchases
* Age Groups vs Purchases
* Commute Distance vs Purchases
* Regional Differences

### Dashboard Creation

Built an interactive dashboard allowing users to filter results using slicers.

## Key Insights

* **Overall, 48.1% of the 1,000 cleaned customer records represented a bicycle purchase.** The dashboard therefore allows both purchasing and non-purchasing customer profiles to be compared across demographic and behavioral characteristics.

* **Purchased-bike customers had a slightly higher average income than non-purchasers.** Average income was **$57,963 among customers who purchased a bike**, compared with **$54,875 among non-purchasers**, a difference of approximately **$3,088 (5.6%)**. The relatively small difference suggests that income alone does not clearly separate buyers from non-buyers in this dataset.

* **Age showed a pronounced difference in purchasing behavior.** Customers in the **Middle Age** group had the highest purchase rate at **54.6%**, compared with **35.5% among Adolescents** and **31.2% among the Old group**. The Middle Age group also accounted for the largest number of actual purchases, with **383 of 701 customers** purchasing a bike.

* **Commute distance showed a clear pattern in purchase rates.** The highest purchase rate occurred among customers commuting **2–5 miles (58.6%)**, followed by **0–1 miles (54.6%)**. Purchase rates then decreased for longer commutes, reaching **39.6% for 5–10 miles** and **29.7% for more than 10 miles**. The difference between the 2–5 mile and more-than-10-mile groups was **28.9 percentage points**.

* **Education was also associated with differences in purchasing behavior.** Customers with a **Bachelor's degree had a purchase rate of 55.2%**, closely followed by customers with a **Graduate Degree at 54.0%**. In comparison, the purchase rate among customers with **Partial High School education was 26.3%**.

* **Marital status showed a noticeable difference in purchase rates.** **Single customers purchased bikes at a rate of 54.1%**, compared with **42.9% among married customers**, a difference of **11.2 percentage points**.

* **Regional differences were visible in the dataset.** Customers in the **Pacific region had the highest purchase rate at 58.9%**, compared with **49.3% in Europe** and **43.3% in North America**.

* **The number of cars owned also showed a pronounced difference in purchasing behavior.** Customers with **no cars had the highest bike purchase rate (61.1%)**, while the rate was **35.6% among customers with four cars**. This corresponds to a difference of **25.5 percentage points**.

* **Gender showed very little difference in overall purchase rates.** **48.9% of female customers** and **47.4% of male customers** purchased a bike. This 1.5-percentage-point difference was substantially smaller than the differences observed across age, commute distance, education, region, or number of cars.

* The interactive dashboard allows these patterns to be examined for specific customer segments using slicers for **Marital Status, Education, Region, and Cars**. This makes it possible to explore how the relationships between income, age, commute distance, and bike purchasing change when focusing on particular customer groups.
